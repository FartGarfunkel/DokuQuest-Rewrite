## What Soulrend is (so nobody re-analyses it)
- A CurseForge modpack folder: `manifest.json`, `modlist.html`, `overrides/`. No mod jars and no combat code.
- `overrides/kubejs/**`: recipes and progression only (plus an estus-flask heal and lifesteal; not taken).
- `overrides/datapacks/BEF_Weapon_Patch.zip`: the one real combat data. Per weapon: blade box (reach, thickness),
  targets per swing, stagger (impact). Medians: sword reach 1.15, 0.4 thick, 2 targets; dagger 0.65 / 0.2 / 1;
  greatsword 1.4 / 0.4 / 4; spear 0.85 / 0.3 / 3. Used in `sim/attack_moves.cpp`.
- The moves and animations themselves are Epic Fight's. Its code is GPL-3 and its clips are "All rights reserved",
  so we measure and write our own (`docs/ANIMATION_STUDY.md`, `docs/CREDITS.md`). Study scripts live outside the repo:
  `~/.hermes/profiles/claudebot/skills/software-development/dokuquest-development/scripts/` (efpose.py with the
  sparse-clone instructions, analyze.py, targets.py, fitarms.py).

## Where combat stands right now (important)
The rules are new and working (Grok, `10896a8`): a click starts a move, the hit lands inside the move's live window.
The DRAWING has not caught up yet:
- The drawn body still uses the old three sword cuts on their own 0.5 s clock (`render/char_layer.cpp` swingU/swingN),
  not `Player::atkMove` / `atkT`. So daggers, axes, fists, tools, the sprint thrust and the air chop show the old
  swing, and the drawn cut is not exactly in step with the hit.
- The blade path in `sim/attack_moves.cpp` (`keysOf`, `fromArcs`) is a placeholder arc per move. The unit tests pass
  (lands ahead, never behind, a level cut misses a chicken, looking down hits it), but it is not yet measured from the
  drawn poses.
- Guests (LAN) don't receive `atkMove/atkT/atkYaw` yet, so other players' swings draw the old way.

## In progress when Matt stopped me (Claude), nothing of it in the repo yet
Fitting our rig to Epic Fight's measured swings (14 clips: sword 1-3, dash, air; dagger 1-3; axe 1-2; fist 1-3;
tool). `targets.py` pulls fist position + blade direction per phase; `fitarms.py` solves our arm angles. Result: with
only arm + forearm angles our sword can't point where theirs does (errors 0.3-0.8 blocks); adding a grip turn (a
"tool joint", 2 angles at the fist) fits every weapon clip to 0.01-0.24 blocks and the blade direction to 0-3 degrees.
Next steps, in order:
1. `render/char_anim.h`: add the grip turn to CharAngles (`toolR`, x and z); `render/char_layer.cpp` heldInHand
   rotates the held item about the fist by it.
2. New `render/attack_poses.cpp`: the fitted keys (our angles, rounded) for all moves, timed from the move table
   (hitFrom = blend + EF preDelay, hitTo = blend + contact, recovery, len); blend in from the stance, hold the
   follow-through, blend back out after recovery. Legs keep walking when moving.
3. `char_layer.cpp`: draw from `p.atkMove/atkT`; the body turns to `atkYaw` for the move (Epic Fight's yaw snap).
4. Shared look-pitch rule (chest share + arm share) used by both the drawing and `attackFrameToWorld`.
5. `test_anim --dump-blades` writes the blade samples from the drawn rig into a generated table for
   `sim/attack_moves.cpp`; a test fails if drawing and table drift apart.
6. Weapon trail ribbon (contact window, fades).
7. Immersive first person for swings: your own arm and weapon doing the third-person cut.
8. Net sync of `atkMove/atkT/atkYaw` to guests (`src/net`, mine).
9. `render/creature_rig.cpp` (Grok handed it to me): mobs show the stagger (snap, hold for `hitStun`, release).
10. Shield check while walking, sprinting, swinging and rolling (standing, looking down and blocking already checked).
